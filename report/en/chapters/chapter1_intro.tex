\chapter{Introduction and Problem Statement}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Background and Motivation}
Weather is an everyday concern for people travelling in Vietnam. Rain, strong winds, heat, and fog can affect visibility, comfort, and journey time. These conditions also influence road safety \cite{andrey2003weather}, particularly for motorcycle riders who are directly exposed to the weather. On longer journeys, conditions at the destination may differ considerably from those along the route.

Travellers can consult navigation applications for directions and weather applications for forecasts. However, planning a journey often requires switching between these services and checking several locations separately. A forecast for the destination alone does not explain what a traveller may encounter on the way. This makes it difficult to compare routes while considering both travel time and weather conditions.

VietForecast addresses this practical need through a web application that brings weather information, an interactive map, and route comparison into one place. Its purpose is to help users understand the available information and make better-informed travel decisions before departure.

\section{Target Users and Their Needs}
The application is intended primarily for individual travellers in Vietnam. Three user groups guide its design:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.25em]
    \item \textbf{Motorcycle riders and commuters} need to check rain, wind, and visibility before travelling. Weather information is especially useful when deciding whether to depart, postpone a trip, or consider another route.
    \item \textbf{Car drivers and families} need an overview of conditions along inter-provincial journeys, together with route distance and estimated travel time. They can use this information to compare the convenience and weather exposure of available alternatives.
    \item \textbf{Tourists and occasional travellers} need an accessible way to explore unfamiliar destinations and check forecasts without interpreting technical meteorological data.
\end{itemize}

These groups share a need for clear information about \textit{where}, \textit{when}, and \textit{how} to travel. The interface therefore prioritizes location search, readable weather summaries, and route comparison. Administrators form a separate operational user group responsible for managing accounts and inspecting the data and services that support the application.

\section{Problem Statement and Project Objectives}
The problem addressed by this project is how to make weather information useful within the process of planning a road journey. A user should be able to select a location or enter an origin and destination, choose a departure time and vehicle type, and understand the conditions relevant to that journey through a single web interface.

The main objective is to develop a usable web application that connects weather exploration with travel planning. Its specific objectives are to:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.25em]
    \item Present current conditions and available forecasts in a consistent, easy-to-read format.
    \item Help users explore weather across Vietnam through a searchable directory and an interactive map.
    \item Compare route alternatives using distance, estimated travel time, and weather assessments for cars and motorcycles.
    \item Make data freshness, missing information, and assessment limitations visible to users.
    \item Support returning users through accounts, saved locations, saved routes, and interface preferences.
\end{enumerate}

The project's main contribution is the integration of these activities into one application. A conversational assistant provides an additional way to ask weather and travel questions; the map, weather pages, and route planner remain the core of the user experience.

\subsection{Checking Weather for a Destination}
A user opens the weather page or map and searches for a destination. The application displays available measurements, such as temperature, rainfall, wind, humidity, and visibility. Selecting a forecast time updates the information, while opening the location details provides a closer view of forecast trends. For example, a tourist considering a visit to Da Nang can check the expected conditions before choosing a day to travel.

\subsection{Comparing Routes Before Departure}
A user enters the starting point and destination, chooses a car or motorcycle, and selects a departure time. The route planner displays available alternatives with their distance, estimated duration, and weather information along the journey. The user can select an alternative on the map and inspect its checkpoints and warnings.

For example, a driver planning a trip from Hanoi to Lao Cai can compare the time and distance of the suggested routes alongside their weather assessments. If weather coverage is incomplete, the interface identifies that limitation so the recommendation can be interpreted with appropriate caution. The available alternatives may share road sections or use the same geometry; they are not guaranteed to be three distinct routes.

\subsection{Returning to Frequently Used Information}
Users can browse the public weather and route pages, while registered users can access personal settings and manage saved locations and routes. Vietnamese and English interfaces, together with light and dark themes, support different reading preferences. Account plans define usage limits for selected services. The assistant offers a supplementary entry point for questions, while the main pages provide the detailed information needed for comparison.

\section{Scope and Limitations}
The project focuses on a browser-based application for road travel within Vietnam, supporting cars and motorcycles. The current dataset uses a 63-province location catalogue. This describes the dataset used by the project rather than a claim that it reflects the latest administrative boundaries. Available forecasts extend up to 72 hours, depending on provider coverage and data freshness.

Weather assessments depend on the measurements available for a location and time. Missing or outdated evidence must be distinguished from low-risk conditions. A weather-safer recommendation represents a comparison of available alternatives under the application's rules; it does not guarantee a safe journey or account for every road hazard. Weather information obtained from external providers is not presented as a direct connection to a national observation network.

The application supports planning and information lookup. Live traffic management, emergency response, and a dedicated mobile turn-by-turn navigation service are outside the project scope. Drivers remain responsible for following road restrictions, official warnings, and conditions observed during travel. Commercial fleet management and the development of a new weather forecasting model are also outside the scope.
